LDB is a **new digital bank**. Credit-transfer fraud in Europe reached 2.2 billion euros in 2024, and an instant payment leaves in ten seconds. Our purpose: stop takeover **before the money leaves**, explain every decision, and work from day one, without any history. (pause) [point to the green cards] Look at the real owner: a device and a phone verified at onboarding, a steady session, a payee paid before. [click: the cards flip red] Now a takeover. Every route has to add **something new**. Phishing brings a new device. A SIM swap changes the phone. A relay makes the session jump. A fake bank caller sends money to a payee never paid before. And a mule is paid by several strangers. A criminal can steal a password, but **cannot make a link old**. [click] Our assumptions: trust starts at verified onboarding, strong authentication already runs on every transfer, we plan with one fraud in 20,000 transfers, and LDB's terms allow a safety hold. Card fraud and the owner's own fraud are out of scope.
Target operating model

The machine decides most. People decide holds.

Fraud lead reads the weekly log, can veto
Risk · 2nd line, monthly report
Audit · 3rd line, every version stored
This is the operating model. [point to the flow] Every transfer passes TrustAge, which scores it in under 0.3 seconds. Below 70 points **the machine decides alone**: it allows, warns, or asks for an extra LuxTrust check. (pause) [click] At **70 points or more** the payment is held, and **a person decides**: two analysts in office hours, the bank's 24/7 desk at night, always with the same four-step call: call the safe phone from onboarding, ask "did you make this payment?", ask open questions, then release, stop, or pause until the next-day deadline. [click] Every answer becomes a **label** that feeds the weekly learning. The fraud lead reads the log and can veto; risk and audit oversee it.
Fraud architecture and components

Three parts inside LDB, one answer in 0.3 s

STEP 1 / 10
Instant payment held? Refused within 10 s, then continues as a standard transfer, decided by D+1.
TrustAge down? Trusted payees still pass; €1,000+ to new payees waits.
Here is the architecture, **all inside LDB**. [the dot follows the payment] The customer orders a transfer. Before sending, the payment hub asks TrustAge: **is this safe?** Part one, the **transaction loader**, collects and checks the bank's records. The **attribute builder** turns them into ages. Part two, the **fraud engine**, scores with the points table and answers in under 0.3 seconds. Every decision is stored for audit. Part three, the **report engine**, shows held payments to the analyst. And outcomes flow back into the weekly recount. [click] Two details: a held instant payment is refused within ten seconds and continues as a standard transfer, decided by the next day. If TrustAge is down, trusted payees still pass.

How does a case get its points? **One formula** for every signal. [point to the formula] F: how many frauds show the signal. G: how many genuine payments show it. [click] Example: a payee added today appears in **70 percent** of frauds but only **8 percent** of genuine payments. 8.75 times more likely, which gives **20 points**. Every 7 points doubles the odds, so 70 points means about a thousand times the odds. That is our hold line. [click] Now a takeover. Phone changed two days ago: 35. New payee: 20. Six times the usual amount: 10. Name only a close match: 10. **75: held** for a call. [click] And an **honest but unusual** payment: 320,000 euros for a flat. The risk signals add up to 65, but it was declared in advance and goes to a notary, so good signs take off 40. **25: paid at once.** [point to the bottom] These signals come from eight rules, five good signs and four fixed overrides, and each signal also sets the **alert priority**: P1 answered within fifteen minutes day and night, P2 within an hour, P3 the next business day.

You asked how the detector adapts **without human intervention**. [point to the loop] Every payment gets an outcome: the analyst's decision, a cancellation, a customer claim, or 60 days without complaint. **Every Sunday night** TrustAge recounts F and G and recomputes the points with the same formula. It is **counting, not machine learning**, so anyone can check it. [click] For example, by month four, 21 of 25 frauds had a fresh payee, so that signal rises **from 20 to 25**, and the log explains why. [click] **Nine safety rails** keep it in check: at most five points a week, floors for core rules, overrides changed only by people, and a new rule starts at zero until the evidence supports it. The fraud lead can always veto.
